Colostrum and the Gut Lining: What the Research Says

By the Gut Save editorial team · Published 25 November 2026 · Educational content, not medical advice

Colostrum is the first milk a cow produces after giving birth. It's very different from regular milk: thicker, and packed with immune proteins, growth factors and other bioactive compounds meant to protect a newborn calf's gut. Researchers have been asking whether those same compounds might help the human intestinal lining.

Why the gut lining matters

Your intestinal lining is a single layer of cells joined by "tight junctions." When it works well, it absorbs nutrients and keeps unwanted material out. Researchers measure how "leaky" it is with tests of intestinal permeability.

What studies have looked at

A review published in PubMed Central, Colostrum Therapy for Human Gastrointestinal Health and Disease, describes how bovine colostrum has been studied for barrier function in animal models and human trials. The authors see a strong scientific rationale and say more work is needed to define its role.

A 2024 meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials looked specifically at bovine colostrum and intestinal permeability in healthy athletes and in patients. Intense exercise is known to stress the gut, which makes athletes a useful group to study.

Keeping it in perspective

  • Most research uses specific colostrum preparations and amounts, which may differ from those in any finished supplement.
  • Positive findings for an ingredient don't prove that a multi-ingredient product works.
  • Colostrum comes from cow's milk, so it isn't suitable for people with milk allergies.

Other ways to look after your gut lining

Eating a fiber-rich diet, limiting alcohol, not smoking and asking your doctor before long-term use of NSAID pain relievers are all sensible, low-cost steps.
